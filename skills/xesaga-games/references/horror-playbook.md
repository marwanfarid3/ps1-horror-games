# Short indie horror playbook

The kind of game this skill is for: short (20-60 minutes), cheap ($0-3), first person, low-res,
one place, one ordinary activity, and something huge and wrong closing in. Players remember the
mood and one image, not the mechanics. Games in this space (Keep an Eye; on Steam, games like
SHIATH, a sub-hour game about self-made doom built around clay pigeon shooting) win reviews on
atmosphere and writing.

Take the genre's conventions, never another game's identity: no copied names, characters, art,
music, levels or exact premises. If the creator names a game he likes, extract *why* it works
(pacing, tone, structure) and build something original from that.

## 1. Find the idea (the formula)
**Ordinary task + unstoppable doom + one impossible image + a rule about looking/listening.**

| Part | Questions | Keep an Eye |
|---|---|---|
| Place | One small place the player learns by heart? | an island with a hut |
| Ordinary task | What would a person do here on a normal day? It must be satisfying on its own. | chop wood, farm, feed goats, build a boat |
| Doom | What is ending, and why can't it be stopped? Who caused it? | a shadow eats the island day by day |
| The image | One picture nobody forgets (wrong scale, wrong anatomy, wrong place) | a giant open hand with an eye in its palm |
| The rule | What does looking, light, sound or silence do? | feeding the eye slows it; watched creatures freeze |
| Theme | What is it really about? (self-made doom, grief, guilt, isolation, denial) | trying to leave what you can't escape |
| Ending | Inevitable? Bitter? A choice between two bad outcomes? | two endings, both lose |

Pitch three different combinations before building; recommend one. Avoid the obvious (zombies,
generic ghosts, jump-scare corridors) unless there's a twist only this game has.

**Stay away from the references.** Don't reuse a reference game's theme, central image type or core
rule by default: if the creator says "like SHIATH", don't make another "self-made doom" game; after
Keep an Eye, don't pitch another giant watcher, eye, feeding-the-threat or freeze-when-watched rule.
Each pitch should differ from every previous studio game in place, task, image and rule.

### How to pitch (idea stage)
- Three ideas, each 4-6 lines: name, place, ordinary task, doom, the image (one sentence a player could
  draw), the rule, the theme, how a run ends. Then one line on why you recommend one.
- Add one honest line per idea on build cost: models needed, crowds/particles/water that weak GPUs
  struggle with, voice acting, anything that needs a paid asset.
- Text first, short, in the creator's language. Once one is picked, make one mood frame (an in-engine
  grey-box screenshot or an inline SVG sketch of the central image) before writing the full plan.
- Draw settings from anywhere in the world: a Nordic lighthouse, a Japanese fishing town, a Texas motel,
  an Egyptian village, a Brazilian river barge, a Scottish moor. Specific places feel more real than
  generic ones. Mix regions across the three pitches unless the creator asks for one, and make sure the
  horror works for a player who knows nothing about that culture.

## 2. Structure: the dread ladder
Split the game into 3-5 acts (days, shifts, nights, rounds). Each act repeats the ordinary task
and climbs one rung:
1. **Normal**: the task, the place, a hint something is off (a sound, a wrong detail far away).
2. **Wrong detail**: the world changed while you slept/looked away. No explanation.
3. **Presence seen**: the threat is visible but does nothing. Being watched.
4. **Presence acts**: it takes something (an animal, a light, a route, health). Rules start to matter.
5. **Collapse**: the task becomes impossible or pointless; the ending plays out.
Make every act shorter and harsher than the last. Repetition is the engine: players notice what
changed because they did the same thing yesterday.

## 3. Techniques that build dread
- **Routine as a lens.** The same chore each act; the horror lives in what's different.
- **The watcher.** Something that looks back. Eyes that track the player are cheap and powerful.
- **Overnight changes.** Footprints, marks, moved objects, a door that was shut. Never explained.
- **Notes/broadcasts.** Short, diegetic, in someone's voice; hint, don't explain. One per act.
- **Looking has a cost.** Things move when unseen, freeze when watched, or punish staring.
- **Safe place violated once.** The hut/room is safe until the one time it isn't.
- **Silence.** Remove ambience before something happens; the absence of birds is a warning.
- **Scale.** Something far too big, too close, too still.
- **Animals as barometers.** They stare, flee, fall silent or die first.
- **One earned scare per act**, set up by sound first. Never repeat a scare; never spam jump scares.

## 4. Look
- 640×360 (or lower) with nearest upscale, low-poly, small nearest-filtered textures, a narrow palette,
  dither + grain, vignette, fog or darkness to hide the edge of the world.
- One accent colour reserved for danger or the central image.
- The threat is readable from the picture alone (a hard shadow line, a light going out, colour drained).

## 5. Sound (half the game)
- Mostly ambience; music only at dawn/act start, at the last light, and in the climax.
- Ambience reacts to danger (insects stop, wind drops, a heartbeat starts).
- Every change in the world has a sound: a tree dying, a door, a body falling, something rising.
- True silence right before the biggest hit. Never touch the player's volume settings for drama.

## 6. Writing
- Short lines, plain words, no lore dumps. Let the player assemble the story.
- A voice: notes by someone who was here before, a radio, a list of chores that goes wrong.
- Ambiguity at the end is fine; confusion about what to *do* is not (prompts stay clear).

## 7. Length, price, store
- 20-60 minutes, two or more endings, a death/attempt counter is a nice touch.
- itch.io free or pay-what-you-want first; Steam at $1.99-$4.99 once there is an audience.
- Trailer: mood first, the ordinary task, the image, the world dying, silence, hit, title.
  No endings. Content warnings for blood, animal death, etc.
- Disclose AI-made assets honestly (Steam and itch.io both ask).

## 8. Checklist before calling an act done
- Can you tell what changed since the last act without reading text?
- Does the threat have one clear rule the player can learn?
- Is there a moment of silence before the act's biggest beat?
- Did the creator's description of the scene appear on screen, beat by beat?
